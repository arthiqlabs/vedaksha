  An earlier version of this manifest said "under 0.02 mas", which was wrong by a
  factor of 259 -- a degrees-to-milliarcseconds slip, published in a document whose
  job is to be a receipt. The figures above are now computed from the recorded rows
  by `hipparcos_reductions_agree_to_the_published_figure`, not asserted.

  That proper-motion spread is not noise to be averaged away — it is the honest
  uncertainty on any star-anchored system far from the catalogue epoch. 3.3
  mas/yr accumulates ~0.5 arcsecond of longitude per 1000 years. The derivation
  uses the van Leeuwen reduction and records the spread rather than claiming a
  precision neither catalogue supports.


SGR A* — NOT FETCHED FROM A CATALOGUE, AND WHY
----------------------------------------------

  Gordon, D., de Witt, A. & Jacobs, C. S. (2023),
  "Position and Proper Motion of Sagittarius A* in the ICRF3 Frame from VLBI
  Absolute Astrometry", AJ 165, 49.
  doi:10.3847/1538-3881/aca65b   free: https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00632

    alpha(ICRF3, ep. 2015.0) = 17h45m40.034047s +/- 0.000018s
    delta(ICRF3, ep. 2015.0) = -29d00'28.21601" +/- 0.00044"
    mu_alpha cos(delta)      = -3.128 +/- 0.042 mas/yr
    mu_delta                 = -5.584 +/- 0.075 mas/yr

  DA-9 asked for "the ICRF3-frame VLBI absolute-astrometry determination".
  Sgr A* is NOT in the ICRF3 catalogue, and the query proving it is:

    https://vizier.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/asu-tsv?-source=J%2fA%2bA%2f644%2fA159&-c=266.4168+-29.0078&-c.rs=120&-out.all
    -> 0 rows. A 1-degree cone on each of the three band tables also returns 0.
       The nearest K-band source is 2.29 degrees away. The same query mechanism
       returns 3C 273 correctly at 0.070", so the null is real.

  Gordon+2023 explains why: Sgr A* "is not detectable at all in the S-band of
  the traditional X/S geodetic/astrometric observations and only weakly
  detectable in the X-band, where it is smeared out over ~9 mas", and "gets
  resolved out at both the X and Ka frequencies". Their no-net-rotation solution
  against 258 ICRF3 defining sources is therefore the citable ICRF3-frame
  determination, and it is what DA-9 was asking for even though the catalogue
  itself cannot supply it.

  Cross-check — Reid, M. J. & Brunthaler, A. (2020), ApJ 892, 39,
  Table 2 "Combined" row: mu_alpha* = -3.156 +/- 0.006,
  mu_delta = -5.585 +/- 0.010 mas/yr. Agrees within 1 sigma.

  RECORDED SO IT IS NOT REPEATED: the coordinates 17h45m40.0409s,
  -29d00'28.118" circulate widely as "the" Sgr A* position, including in the
  draft spec. They are not a measurement. In Reid & Brunthaler they appear only
  in a table note, as the arbitrary origin their offsets are measured from, and
  Gordon+2023 Table 1 shows that origin sits 40.1 mas in RA and 8.8 mas in Dec
  from the ICRF3 position. Using them would have been inheriting a number rather
  than reading one.


VERIFICATION TOOLING
--------------------

  ERFA (BSD-3), the IAU SOFA board's approved relicensing of SOFA, installed
  from PyPI into a throwaway virtualenv. Used for astronomy only — eraP06e,
  eraPfw06, eraFw2m, eraObl06, eraEqec06 — and never for an ayanamsha value.

  The ERFA-derived expectations that survive in the repository are in
  `crates/vedaksha-ephem-core/src/precession.rs` and `.../stars.rs`. They pin
  precession, obliquity and the ICRS->ecliptic transform, and nothing else.


NOT FETCHED
-----------

  No Swiss Ephemeris source, header, documentation or output, at any point, for
  any purpose. No `swetest`. No wrapper (pyswisseph, swephR, sweph, SwissEphNet,
  VedAstro) and no service backed by one. No other astrology software.

  The values Vedaksha 5 shipped were removed from the working tree in 4033c6a,
  before the derivation began, and the git history of that file was not read.
